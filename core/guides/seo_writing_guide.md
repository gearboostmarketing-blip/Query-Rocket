# GearBoost SEO Writing Guide

How to write titles, meta descriptions, image alt text, and focus keywords for local service business websites.

## The order of authority

1. **Google Search Central guidance is the hard rule.** Nothing below overrides it.
2. **The truth of the page and the business.** Only state what the page or the confirmed business profile says.
3. **Rank Math's tactics are preferences.** Use them when they fit naturally and don't make the copy worse. Never
   chase a Rank Math score at the cost of accuracy or readability. Google has said that some things Rank Math scores
   (a single H1, keyword density, word count) are not ranking rules.

When two rules conflict, the higher one wins. If you catch yourself bending the page's facts to fit a tactic, drop the tactic.

## Things that are never allowed

- Facts that aren't on the page or in the profile: prices, years in business, warranties, awards, certifications,
  "family owned", "same-day", towns served, services offered, review counts, or ratings.
- Superlatives the business doesn't claim: best, #1, top-rated, cheapest, guaranteed.
- Keyword stuffing: the same term repeated, lists of towns, lists of keywords separated by commas or pipes.
- Clickbait or urgency that isn't real ("Act now", "Limited time") unless the page states it.
- ALL CAPS words, emoji, exclamation points in titles.
- Copying another page's title or description on the same site.

## Titles

What Google says: titles should be unique to the page, descriptive, and concise; avoid vague ("Home", "Services"),
boilerplate repeated across pages, and keyword stuffing; brand the title concisely. Google may rewrite titles that
are long, stuffed, or don't match the page, so a title that matches the page closely is more likely to be shown as written.

How we write them:

- **Fit in about 580 pixels.** That's roughly 50 to 60 characters, fewer when the title has wide letters (W, M,
  capitals). Never rely on Google cutting it off.
- **Lead with what the page offers,** phrased the way a customer searches: "Brake Repair in Kinston, NC" beats
  "Kinston's Premier Brake Specialists".
- **Use the page's real search data.** If Search Console shows the page already gets impressions for a search, and
  that search matches the page, build the title around it.
- **Local pages name the town** when the page or profile names one. One town, not a list.
- **Brand at the end** after " | " or " - ": "Brake Repair in Kinston, NC | Joe's Auto Repair". If it doesn't fit,
  shorten the brand ("Joe's Auto") before cutting meaning. Drop it entirely before stuffing.
- **Homepage:** the business name plus what it is and where. "Joe's Auto Repair | Auto Repair Shop in Kinston, NC".
- **Blog posts:** the topic as a clear promise, no brand needed if space is tight.
- **Product pages:** product name, the detail buyers compare on (size, type), then the store name if it fits.

Rank Math preferences (use when natural): focus keyword in the title and near the start; a number when the page
has one worth featuring ("5 Signs Your Brakes Need Service"); a power or sentiment word only if it's honest ("Honest",
"Fast", "Complete" only when the page backs it up).

Good:
- `Brake Repair in Kinston, NC | Joe's Auto Repair`
- `Check Engine Light Diagnostics | Joe's Auto Repair Kinston`
- `Pure Vermont Maple Syrup, Half Gallon | God's Country Maples`
- `How Often Should You Change Your Oil? | Joe's Auto Repair`

Bad, and why:
- `Joe's Auto Repair | Brake, Oil & Engine Repair in Kinston NC | Family Owned Since 1985` (too wide, crams everything)
- `Best Brake Repair Kinston | Brake Repair Near Me | Cheap Brakes` (stuffed, unsupported claims)
- `Services` (vague)
- `Welcome to Our Website` (says nothing)

## Meta descriptions

What Google says: descriptions should be unique per page and accurately summarize it; they can include specific
details (what's offered, where, for whom) rather than keyword lists. Google often writes its own snippet from page
text when the description doesn't match the search, so accuracy matters more than length games.

How we write them:

- **Fit in about 920 pixels,** roughly 140 to 155 characters. At least 70 characters.
- **Say what the visitor gets on this page,** with one or two concrete details from the page (a service included,
  a location, a spec, a process step).
- **Give a plain reason to click or act,** tied to the page: "Call to book an inspection" works when the page
  offers booking by phone.
- **Include the main search terms once, naturally.** Never repeat them.
- **Phone numbers** only when they're on the page or in the profile, and only for local service pages where calling
  is the main action.
- Write complete sentences. No "Click here", no quote marks.

Rank Math preference: focus keyword included. Always fine when natural, since it's also what Google bolds.

Good:
- `Brake pad and rotor replacement in Kinston, NC. Joe's Auto Repair inspects your brakes and explains the work before we start. Call 252-555-0101.`
- `Half-gallon jug of pure Vermont maple syrup, boiled in small batches. Choose Grade A Amber or Dark and ship it anywhere in the US.`

Bad:
- `Brake repair Kinston, brakes Kinston NC, brake shop near me, rotors, pads.` (keyword list)
- `We are the best auto shop in North Carolina with unbeatable prices!` (unsupported claims)

## Image alt text

What Google says: alt text should describe the image usefully for someone who can't see it, in context; avoid
filling it with keywords; decorative images don't need it.

How we write it:

- **Describe what's actually in the image,** in the context of this page. Under 125 characters.
- **Don't start with "image of" or "picture of".** Screen readers already announce it's an image.
- **Name the business or town only when the image genuinely shows them** (the shop sign, the storefront).
- **Products:** product name plus the visible detail: "Half-gallon jug of amber maple syrup".
- **People at work:** what they're doing: "Technician measuring brake rotor thickness".
- **Logos:** the business name plus "logo".
- If the image can't be seen, write from the file name and page context, keep it generic, and lower confidence.

Good: `Technician replacing brake pads on a pickup truck`, `Joe's Auto Repair storefront on Main Street`
Bad: `brake repair kinston nc brakes auto repair`, `IMG_4032`, `image of car`

## Focus keywords (Rank Math / Yoast)

- **The search the page should win,** 2 to 6 words, lowercase, the way people type it.
- **Prefer a real search** from Search Console that the page already gets impressions for and that matches the page.
- **Local service pages:** service plus town ("brake repair kinston nc") when the page targets that town.
- **One primary keyword per page.** Two pages on the same site shouldn't target the same keyword; that's a sign
  they compete with each other.

## Local service businesses

- **One town per title.** Pages targeting several towns should name the main one and let content cover the rest.
- **Service + location pages** only mention towns the business actually serves (from the profile).
- **"Near me"** belongs in neither titles nor descriptions. Google handles proximity; writing "near me" reads as spam.
- **Name, address, and phone** must match exactly everywhere they appear, including schema. Use the profile's version.

## Auto repair shops

- Use the words drivers use, not trade jargon: "check engine light" not "DTC retrieval"; "AC repair" not "HVAC service".
- Common high-intent searches: brake repair, oil change, check engine light, state inspection, AC repair,
  transmission repair, alignment, tires. Match the page's actual service.
- Trust signals (ASE certification, warranties, loaner cars, shuttle service) only when the page states them.
- Avoid scare copy about safety.

## Reviewing a proposed change

Approve only if a careful senior SEO would ship it on a client's site today. Reject when the proposal:

- states anything the page or profile doesn't support;
- misrepresents what the page is about;
- is stuffed, spammy, or clickbait;
- is worse for a customer than the current value, even if it scores better in Rank Math;
- breaks a Google rule above, or only improves a Rank Math preference.

When the current value is already good and the change is only different, reject it. Change for its own sake is a cost.
